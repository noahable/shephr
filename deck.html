The Problem
Four gaps.
One platform closes them all.
Gap 01
Every wrong decision is a direct commercial event.
A misjudged cancellation doesn't just cost the recovery — it destroys the revenue the seat was carrying, triggers EU261 compensation exposure, and forces rebooking leakage onto competitor metal. A missed crew positioning window costs the next four rotations. These are not operational inconveniences. They are P&L line items that land immediately, compound quickly, and are rarely attributed correctly to the decision that caused them.
Gap 02
Data is not the same as foresight.
Airlines and airports are not short of information. Dashboards are live. Alerts are firing. But no existing system simulates the downstream consequence of each option — delay vs. cancel, hold vs. swap, protect connections vs. preserve hub bank. The signal exists. The translation into ranked, actionable decisions does not. Controllers are forced to act on instinct, under time pressure, with no view of what happens next.
Gap 03
Every stakeholder optimises locally. The system fails globally.
Airports are not one operation — they are ten simultaneous operations sharing the same infrastructure. When disruption hits, the airline protects its hub bank, the ground handler protects its SLA, ATC protects slot compliance, and security protects throughput. Nobody is optimising the whole. The result is a system that doesn't fail at one point — it unravels across all of them at once, with each actor certain they made the right local call.
Gap 04
Everyone else rehearses. Airports and airlines never have.
ATC candidates complete mandatory simulation before touching live traffic. Pilots train on type before their first line flight. Signallers rehearse on simulators before managing live rail movements. Airport operations controllers and OCC teams — managing the most complex multi-stakeholder decision environment in commercial aviation — have never had an equivalent. The gap isn't a training gap. It's the complete absence of a rehearsal discipline, and every disruption season proves the cost of that.
Shepherd closes all four in a single platform — converting disruption signals into ranked decisions, and building the trained instinct to act on them correctly.
